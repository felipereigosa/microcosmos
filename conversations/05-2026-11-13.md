## Claude

My pick is **Gonium pectorale**.

It is a flat, rounded-square plate of 16 translucent green cells (a 4-cell centre ringed by 12), held in clear jelly. It swims slowly while spinning like a frisbee, and wobbles and tumbles when it turns, which is the motion you describe.

- **Paper:** de Maleprade, Moisy, Ishikawa and Goldstein, "Motility and phototaxis of Gonium, the simplest differentiated colonial alga" (Physical Review E, 2020). It gives the colony geometry, how the flagella are arranged, and the swimming and spin rates, and it comes with videos. I am citing this from memory and have not pulled it yet, so I will check the numbers when I do.
- **Insides:** each cell is essentially a Chlamydomonas, the best-documented algal cell there is. It has one big cup-shaped chloroplast with a pyrenoid, a red eyespot, a nucleus, two contractile vacuoles and two flagella, so the close-up organelles can be textbook-accurate.
- **Caveat:** the "boxy" look comes from the colony's shape and the cells' angular packing, not from thick rectangular walls.

If you want literal boxes, look at these too:

- **Scenedesmus / Desmodesmus:** 4 or 8 oblong cells side by side like a little raft, often with corner spines. It is the boxiest option, but it has no flagella, so it only drifts and tumbles in the current.
- **Pediastrum:** a flat star-shaped gear of angular cells, very geometric, and also a passive drifter.
- **Micrasterias:** a single large, ornate desmid, a flat disc with deeply cut lobes. It is gorgeous but nearly motionless.
- **Phacus:** a single rigid, leaf-shaped green cell that swims in a lazy corkscrew. It is not boxy, but it matches "swimming lazily" best after Gonium.

Search "Gonium pectorale microscope" and "Gonium swimming" (video is worth it for the motion), then "Scenedesmus quadricauda" and "Pediastrum" for comparison. Tell me which you like and I will fetch the paper and footage and start the model.

Four Desmodesmus colonies now drift and tumble in the drop alongside the Lacrymaria. Reload `web/microcosm.html` in Firefox on the laptop to see them. I checked it by screenshot only (far, mid and close), so the look held up for me but the motion is yours to judge.

- **The paper:** Hegewald and Braband, "A taxonomic revision of Desmodesmus serie Desmodesmus", Fottea 2017 (open access, saved in `references/desmodesmus/`). The species is *Desmodesmus communis*, the classic four-spined "Scenedesmus quadricauda".
- **From the paper:** cell length and proportions, rows of 2, 4 or 8 cells, the four corner spines and their length, the net on the outer wall, and the tiny rosettes of 4 or 5 tubes on the wall.
- **Not from the paper:** it is a paper about the wall, so the inside is less exact than for Lacrymaria. One chloroplast lining the wall with one pyrenoid is the genus description. The nucleus, Golgi, mitochondria, vacuoles, starch and oil drops are generic green-alga anatomy that I placed by hand.
- **The better source I could not get:** the classic ultrastructure paper (Staehelin and Pickett-Heaps, Journal of Phycology 1975) is paywalled. If you can get the PDF, I will correct the inside against it.
- **The look:** the boxy see-through cells with a lobed bright green chloroplast are matched to a light micrograph from Wikimedia Commons. Close up, the wall net, thylakoid lines and small organelles fade in, as with the Lacrymaria. The net is drawn several times coarser than in life so it can be seen.
- **The motion:** Desmodesmus has no flagella, so it cannot swim. Each colony wanders slowly around its spot and turns over and over. That motion is invented, with no footage behind it. It loops with the rest, and the colonies stay clear of the debris, the filaments and the animal.
- **Small extras:** clicking an alga sets the orbit pivot, and the credit line cites the paper. The page title still says only "Lacrymaria olor".
