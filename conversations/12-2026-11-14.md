## Claude

Ray plus click is the right way to select, but I'd avoid the big floating panel: text on the Quest 2 is coarse, and a wall of it pulls your eyes off the organism, which is the whole point of being in there.

What I'd build instead, in order of preference:

1. **Labels on the organism itself.** Clicking shows a small tag beside it with the name and one line (size, what it eats), plus thin leader lines to its parts: "macronucleus", "contractile vacuole", "oral groove". The information sits where the thing is, and it suits the textbook organelles you already have up close.
2. **The full text on your wrist.** The longer description lives on a card attached to the left controller, so you raise your hand like a watch to read it and lower it to make it go away.
3. **Narration.** Clicking plays a short spoken version of the info text, documentary style, so your eyes never leave the animal. It is the most comfortable option in VR, but it needs audio clips generated per organism, and it is a bigger change in feel.

My recommendation is 1 and 2 together: ray to point, trigger to select and follow (same as clicking on desktop), tag and part labels on the organism, full text on the wrist. Narration could come later on top without changing any of that.

## Claude

Yes: the **point of a sewing needle** (or a pin). You were closer than I gave you credit for. What I ruled out was the hypodermic needle, which is a hollow tube 0.2 to 0.3 mm wide with a long slanted cut. A sewing needle is a solid cone that comes to a point only a few tens of micrometres across, so the very tip is about the size of a Halteria and the steel widens away into the dark beyond the edge of the scene.

Why I think it fits:
- **Simple to make convincing:** it is one polished metal cone with a slightly rounded point and fine grinding lines running round it. There is none of the organic detail that worried you about the hair.
- **Instantly recognisable:** bright steel tapering to a point reads as a needle from any angle.
- **Scale everyone has felt:** people know a needle point as the sharpest, smallest thing they handle, so seeing a paramecium that could sit on it does the job at a glance.
- **Stays in the background:** only a glint along one edge needs to catch the light; the rest can sit in shadow until you fly up to it.

Two other artificial options, both weaker:
- **The eye of a needle:** about 0.4 mm by 1 mm, so the whole scene would nearly fit through it. It is a nice idea, but it is a frame around everything rather than a quiet object at the edge.
- **A 0.5 mm pencil lead:** the one object here whose size people know as a number, but it is a plain dark cylinder five times thicker than the Lacrymaria's body, and hard to recognise without its pencil.

Shall I build the needle point?
