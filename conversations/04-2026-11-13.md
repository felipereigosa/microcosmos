## Claude

First try is built and on your laptop: reload `web/microcosm.html` in Firefox and zoom into the body. I checked it by screenshot only, so the look and the motion are yours to judge.

From the default view nothing changes; I compared screenshots before and after. The detail starts to fade in at about 230 µm from the camera and is all there within 90 µm.

What appears as you come closer, in the same see-through style:

- **Macronuclei:** pores in the envelope, chromatin grains and nucleoli inside, and blue-violet sheets of rough reticulum with ribosome dots folded round one side.
- **Micronucleus:** a dense core.
- **Mitochondria:** 52 coral capsules under the wall, with cristae showing through. These came out best.
- **Golgi:** six golden stacks of bowed discs with vesicles around them. They read well edge-on and only as concentric rings face-on.
- **Contractile vacuoles:** feeder canals with swollen ampullae.
- **Food vacuoles:** prey inside, green when fresh and browner when older, with violet lysosomes around.
- **Head:** a basket of rods running back from the mouth.
- **Cilia:** a small golden basal body at the foot of each one, which traces the pleats.

One change is visible at any distance: the contractile vacuoles now pulse. They fill slowly and empty at a stroke, every 4.6 and 6.1 s of the animal's time, and the canals swell as the vacuole empties. Say so if you would rather they stayed still from afar.

Two things to know:

- **Granule beads:** the old granule beads are untouched, and up close they hide a fair amount of the new parts. Thinning or shrinking them when the camera is near would give the organelles more room; I held back because it changes the existing look.
- **Frame rate:** in the test Chromium it was 39 to 47 fps close up with or without the detail, so no measurable cost, but that measurement is noisy. I did not measure Firefox.

Nothing is committed. `PLAN.md` has the notes.

## Claude

Done: a left click on the animal now makes that spot the camera pivot. Reload `web/microcosm.html` to try it.

- **What a click does:** the view slides over in about half a second until the clicked spot is in the middle of the screen, and orbiting and zooming then go about it. The camera keeps its angle and distance.
- **What counts as a click:** left button, released within half a second and without moving more than 5 pixels, so dragging to orbit is unaffected. The cilia and a few pixels around the outline count as the animal.
- **Open water:** a click that misses the animal does nothing.
- **The pivot stays put:** it does not follow the animal as it moves on, in keeping with the fixed camera.

I tested it with simulated clicks in the test Chromium: body and neck clicks moved the pivot to the right place, and a click on empty water left it alone. I have not tried it with a real mouse in Firefox.

I also added "click the animal to orbit about that spot" to the hint under the title. Nothing is committed.
